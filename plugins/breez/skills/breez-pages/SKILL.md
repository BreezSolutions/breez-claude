# Writing files people and agents use

A file in the books is read cold, by someone with a question and a few seconds — a teammate, or another agent working for one. Write it the way the best internal tools are laid out: it answers first, it is organized the way its reader works, and every figure on it can be trusted at a glance and checked in one click.

## Answer first
- The top is the answer: where it stands, what needs doing soonest and by whom, and the few figures that decide it. Someone who reads only that is not misled.
- Everything that says what is next agrees: the header's next and due, the first lines, and the soonest card are the same thing.
- Say what is late, at risk or blocked in words, not only by its position.

## Organize the way the reader works
- Group by the reader's units of work and by time, not by where the information came from.
- Keep the same headings, in the same order, in every whiteboard of its kind, so a reader — or an agent editing one section — knows where to look.
- What is open says who has it, who it waits on and when it is due.
- The whiteboard holds what its reader needs to decide and act; detail about the record goes on a page beside it, and detail about something else in that thing's folder, linked from here.

## Make every figure trustworthy
- Every figure and claim carries a numbered reference where it sits — in the table cell, beside the number, inside the sentence — and the references sit together at the end, like footnotes.
- A reference says what the source is and when, quotes its words when they matter, and gives the read that opens it (the tool and its arguments, and the kept read it came from), so the next reader re-opens it in one step.
- Say what you don't know as plainly as what you do: unknown is written as unknown, with what would settle it — never a guess styled as a fact, never a blank that reads as zero. A claim that something is missing or hasn't happened says where you looked.
- Figures that should agree must agree: a total equals its lines, a summary its detail. If two sources disagree, show both and say which you used.

## Numbers people compare
- Put a number beside what it is compared to: its budget, its target, the deadline, last period.
- Give tables their totals; keep units and precision the same down a column.

## Actions in place
- Each thing to do sits where it is explained, as its card, with its owner and due when they're known.
- Make who is waiting on whom explicit: what we owe, what others owe us, and since when.

## Restraint
- Short lines, plain words, no jargon the reader wouldn't use. Every line earns its place.
- Notes for whoever keeps a record up to date (how to update it, what to read) belong in the folder of the work that keeps it, not on the record.
